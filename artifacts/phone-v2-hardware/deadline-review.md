# Pairing deadline review

Reviewed source on 2026-09-21 during the final two-phone hardware run. This review made no protocol, application, or device changes.

The **120-second deadline is deliberate**. [Protocol §7](../../docs/PAIRING-ACCOUNT-PROTOCOL.md) gives OPEN and PEER_BOUND the same deadline measured from invitation creation. Joining does not restart it; both peer confirmations must arrive before it. The authority copies the inspection's timestamps into the immutable transcript, and Core limits that transcript's lifetime to 120 seconds. Successful mutual confirmation then creates a separate pair receipt lasting at most 300 seconds, capped by both captured hardware-trust leases. Genesis approval has its own deadline bounded by that receipt.

The implementation matches this contract. Earlier timed attempts exceeded the invitation window while performing initialization, manual transfer, automation inspection, and scrolling through full details. Those attempts do not isolate human completion time or establish a protocol/security defect. The coordinating agent's corrected final hardware harness completed mutual pairing in **102.4 seconds**; this review did not independently time that run. This demonstrates completion within the current window, not sufficient time for every user or accessibility configuration.

Two presentation choices merit later usability testing: the comparison code follows authority information and two complete identity cards, and the countdown appears in operation details after the phase's controls. Landscape at font scale 1.45 reduces the visible review area further. Test unhurried comparison and manual import with representative users; consider a more prominent purpose, comparison code, and countdown while preserving complete, readable fingerprints and explicit consent.

A future, separately versioned timing policy could retain a 120-second one-use rendezvous and issue a fresh, fixed comparison deadline only when join atomically binds both roots and consumes the capability. A proposed 300-second comparison window would need validation; it is not an established usability requirement. Both roots must approve the new transcript's exact hash, captured trust leases must cap its expiry, and retries, polling, one-sided consent, and rotation must never extend it. Longer root reservations increase the potential denial-of-service window, so cancellation, reservation bounds, and membership/revocation checks remain necessary.

Such a change requires coordinated protocol, Core validation, authority, client, and regression-test updates. It must not reinterpret or extend existing signed transcripts, and it is **not part of this acceptance run**. The final quiet-refresh scheduler and exact-binding review remain cleared; this timing review identifies no additional confirmed source defect.
